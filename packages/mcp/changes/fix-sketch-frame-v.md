### Fixed

- `slicerx_geom_extrude` 1.12.4, `slicerx_geom_revolve` 1.10.2, `slicerx_geom_loft` 1.13.1, `slicerx_geom_sweep` 1.11.2, `slicerx_design_sketch` 1.4.1, `slicerx_design_sketch_svg` 1.1.1 and `slicerx_design_trace_image` 1.1.1 keep the `v` of a sketch frame. Before, `v` was read as normal × u whatever was given, so on a side face with `u` along X and `v` up, a sketch drawn at height 11 landed at height -11 and a cut there was refused as stopping short of the part. A `v` that runs opposite normal × u now mirrors the sketch onto the step's frame, so every point lands where the frame given puts it, on mesh and exact designs alike, and the answer ends with "The frame's v [0, 0, 1] runs opposite normal × u, so the sketch was mirrored onto the step's frame and each point stays where this frame put it." Each axis is still scaled to 1 long. A `v` that is not at right angles to `u` and the normal, within 0.0001, is refused with "frame: with v given, u, v and normal must be at right angles to each other, within 0.0001; here v and normal meet at 84.289 degrees. Fix them, or leave v out and v is normal × u." Text, SVG artwork and a traced image on a frame whose `v` runs opposite normal × u would read backwards, so they are refused with "frame: v [0, 0, 1] runs against normal × u ([0, 0, -1] here), so this plane is seen from behind and the text would read backwards. Keep v and give u as v × normal ([-1, 0, 0]), or leave v out." An angle constraint that follows a value (`expr`) on such a frame is refused with "constraints.2.expr: the frame's v runs against normal × u, so the sketch is mirrored onto v = normal × u and its angles turn the other way, which an angle that follows a value cannot do. Give the angle as a number, or v as normal × u." The frame's description says "v is normal × u unless given".
- `slicerx_geom_rail` 1.1.0 takes `v` in its path's frame and keeps it as `slicerx_geom_extrude` does. Before, a `v` given there was dropped.
