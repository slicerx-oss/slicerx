## [0.2.10] - 2026-11-18

### Breaking

- `slicerx_geom_edge_pick` 2.0.0 (from 1.5.0) returns `loopRef` and `chainRef` first, the face's loop and the edge's tangent chain as one entry each that `slicerx_geom_fillet` and `slicerx_geom_chamfer` take as is, with `loopEdges` and `chainEdges` (how many edges each names). The per-edge `chain` and `loop` lists come only with `list_edges: true`; before, every pick returned them.
- `slicerx_geom_hole` 2.0.0 (from 1.3.0) cuts a new round hole in a model or a design. Changing a hole already there moved to `slicerx_geom_hole_edit` (see Added). Place the hole by `center`, its center on the flat face it starts at or any point on its axis line, with `axis` either way round (left out, square to the face at `center`), or by `face`, `{at, normal?}`, a point on that face for a hole square to it. `diameter_mm` is required. Without `depth_mm` the hole goes through the wall it starts in; `counterbore` or `countersink` puts a head at its entry. The hole starts where its axis line meets the part nearest the point, and its whole circle, and the head's, must sit on a flat face square to the axis: a point on a round face (a boss's side), a circle that runs off the face, an axis more than 1 degree off square, a line that meets no part and a hole already on that axis where the new one would start are each refused with `invalid_input`, the last naming the hole and `slicerx_geom_hole_edit`, and nothing changes. The answer has `hole`, the hole as made (`center`, `axis`, `diameterMm`, `depthMm`, `kind`, `head`), in place of `before` and `after`. On a design the hole is a `shape.extrude` step, a circle on the entry face cut into it as Extrude with Cut makes it, named by `label` (default `Hole <diameter> mm`), and a head is a second cut on the same face after it, named `Counterbore <diameter> mm` or `Countersink <diameter> mm`: a wider circle as deep as the counterbore, or for a countersink a circle narrowing to the hole, its sides drafted by half the cone's angle. A countersink's `angle_deg` is 30 to 90 (default 90), and its cone must end above the hole's far end. When the head cannot go on, the cut is taken back off and the call is refused. A hole the engine cannot read back as one (it runs out through a round face, say) keeps its steps, and `hole` is null with a note in the summary. With a model it writes the new STL.

### Added

- `slicerx_project_mixed_filaments` 1.0.0 lists the open project's mixed filaments: each one's `id` (the filament number objects and paint use, 29 to 32), `name`, its two loaded filaments (`components`), the `ratio` in layers, the first `cycle` of filaments layer by layer, and an approximate `color` with the two filaments' `colors`.
- `slicerx_project_add_mixed_filament` 1.0.0 adds a mix of two loaded filaments with a ratio in layers (each side 1 to 20). It takes the highest free number from 32 down, four mixes at most, and answers with the mix and the project's mixes read back. A slice prints each layer of a region on the mix in one of the two filaments, the second's layers spread through each cycle (2:1 prints A, A, B).
- `slicerx_project_set_mixed_filament` 1.0.0 changes a mix's filaments, ratio or name, and `slicerx_project_remove_mixed_filament` 1.0.0 deletes one, the objects on it going back to its first filament; both answer with the mixes read back.
- `slicerx_project_set_object_filament` 1.0.0 prints an object with a loaded filament or a mix by its number, and answers with the object's filaments as the slice reads them.
- `slicerx_app_mixed_filaments` 1.0.0 reads the mixes of the project open in the app, and `slicerx_app_mixed_filaments_set` 1.0.0 sets them as a whole list in one Undo step, a mix the list leaves out taking its parts and paint back to its first filament.
- `slicerx_app_look` 1.2.0 reads `spaceMouse`, the 3D mouse setting from Settings, Controls: `enabled`, `sensitivity` (0.25 to 4), `invert` (`panX`, `panY`, `zoom`, `tilt`, `spin`), `dominantAxis`, and `status` (`unsupported`, `off`, `none`, `connected`, `busy` when the device could not be opened, often because its maker's driver keeps it, or `error`) with the `device` name. `slicerx_app_look_set` 1.1.0 takes `spaceMouse` with any of those fields; one left out keeps its value. Turned on, the 3D mouse only moves the camera and presses the view's reset and fit buttons. In the desktop app that starts reading a plugged-in device at once; in a browser only the person can connect a new device, since the browser asks them.
- `slicerx_design_render` 1.0.0 draws a design's parts, whole or as an exploded view: `spread` (0 to 100 percent) moves them apart radially (`axis` `radial`, the default) or along a bed axis (`x`, `y`, `z`, where the lowest part stays), with the same offsets as the app's Spread control. It answers `offsetsMm` per part and the PNG with `image: true`; the design is not changed.
- `slicerx_app_explode` 1.0.0 and `slicerx_app_explode_set` 1.0.0 read and set the open app's exploded view through the app link (new methods `explode.get` and `explode.set`, read class): `spread`, `axis`, the selected objects of 2 parts or more it spreads (`targets`), the offsets the view draws their parts at (`drawn`), whether it draws them now (`shown`) and whether an edit tool holds the parts where they print (`held`). `slicerx_app_explode_set` answers `busy` while the toolpaths are shown or an edit tool is open. Display only: the project, the slice and the undo history never see it.
- `slicerx_app_feature_filaments` 1.0.0 reads the open app's feature filament table (the Color tab): for the shown plate, or with `objects` for those objects, one row per feature (outer and inner wall, sparse and solid infill, top and bottom surface, support and support interface) with its `feature`, `label`, `key`, `value` (0 is the object's own filament, null when the objects differ), `source` (`plate`, `own` or `mixed`), `changed` and `printedWith`, the filament slots the feature printed with in the current slice (null with no current slice).
- `slicerx_app_feature_filaments_set` 1.0.0 sets which filament prints each feature, as a pick in that table does: `filaments` by feature name or setting key, 0 for the object's own filament, a slot from 1, or null to go back to the inherited value; for the plate, or with `objects` for those objects, each source object once. Every row is checked before any change, and a filament past the project's filaments is refused with `invalid_input`. It answers with the table as `slicerx_app_feature_filaments` reads it.
- `slicerx_paint_preview` 1.6.0 (from 1.5.0) takes the target `{ "kind": "feature", "feature": "<name>" }`: every face the steps of that plan feature made, on a body built from many steps. The feature's steps are the steps tagged with it, every row or mirror of them (`slicerx_geom_array` or `slicerx_geom_mirror` with `steps`), and the steps a row tagged with it repeats. The walls and floor a cut makes belong to the cut's feature, and a face it cuts through stays its own feature's. The name matches case and spacing aside. A name the design has no step for is refused with `invalid_input`, listing the feature names it has. `slicerx_paint_apply` adds it as a paint step that finds the feature's faces again on every replay. On exact solids, a face that a join clears the key of (one a joined shape sits flat on, or one a mirror image lands flush with) goes by the key it came from, so it paints with the feature that made it: the top of a tower around a pole joined on it paints with the tower, and a mirrored boss paints whole on both sides. A part built as a mesh, in a design that is not exact or from the step where a part grew too detailed to stay exact, is refused with `invalid_input`: "target: painting by feature needs a part built as an exact solid, and part N is not one; paint it by height, by direction or from a seed point instead".
- `slicerx_app_paint_preview` 1.2.0 (from 1.1.0) takes the same feature target on an object in the app, by the steps of its history.
- `slicerx_geom_extrude` 1.14.0 (from 1.13.0): a `rectangle` shape takes `frontRadiusMm` and `backRadiusMm`, which round only the two corners at its +x (front) or -x (back) end, each up to half the height. At half the height that end is one half circle. An end's radius takes the place of `cornerRadiusMm` at that end, so a rectangle with `cornerRadiusMm` 2 and `frontRadiusMm` 10 has a round front and small rounds at the back. A radius past half the height is refused with "frontRadiusMm: between 0 and half the height" (or backRadiusMm), and two ends that need more than the width with "frontRadiusMm: together with the other end's radius, at most the width". On an exact design the rounds are arcs of the outline, as `cornerRadiusMm`'s are. `cornerRadiusMm` is now described in the schema: it rounds all four corners, up to half the shorter side.
- `slicerx_design_plan` 1.20.0 (from 1.19.0), in the mimir tab: each body in `flat` takes `ends`, how its levels' ends and corners are shaped: `"round"`, `"square"`, or `{"front": ..., "back": ...}`. Left out at display and fine detail it is round. The plan keeps it. Each flat body's `levels` says that, at display and fine detail, each level that carries another level has a thin roof of its own, a layer or two thick, about one nozzle width past its own walls all round, built with the level, however far the level above is set in; a post, leg, pole or handle standing on a level adds no roof.
- `slicerx_design_check` 1.26.0 (from 1.25.0): for a display or fine plan, a flat body whose ends are round (as planned, or left out) and whose every level was built square (plain rectangles, straight-sided sketches or boxes, with no corner or end radius and no fillet) gets a note: "Every level of <body> has square ends. ...". It is a note beside the check, never a failed item. A level with any rounded step, or built another way, gets none. The result's new `suggest` holds the same notes as they are passed on to the person, in plain words with no setting names ("<body> has square corners; I can round its corners or ends if the real one is rounded."), and a passing check's summary passes those on in place of `notes`.
- `slicerx_design_lookup_sizes` 1.1.1, in the mimir tab: looks a real thing's sizes up on the web before mimir plans a model of it. It takes the thing's name and at most 5 sizes to find, the ones sources commonly publish, and returns a lookupId, each size found in mm with what the source said, the sizes no source gave, and the source pages, the pages its answer cites, shown to the person as a lookup card. The search runs with thinking off, so its output goes to the answer, and asks for each line to start with the size's name. A line is read by its exact name, then by a name with the same head noun ("Width" for "Width (across)"), then, when no line names a size, one line per size in the order asked; a figure given in meters and in feet is read by its meters. An answer that ran no search finds no sizes ("found 0 of 3 sizes; no source gave ..."), since nothing was read off the web, and its lookup card shows none. A search that ran always counts, even when the answer cites no page; its lookup card then links no page, since no page the answer did not cite is shown as its source. A local model is not offered it, and a slicing or printer turn at the tool limit leaves it out. Where no web lookup can run it says so: "Web lookup is not available here, so give the real sizes from what you know; the plan says they were not looked up".
- `slicerx_design_get_plan` 1.0.0 reads a kept plan back by its planId, as `slicerx_design_check` holds the part to it: its features and sizes, its scale and real sizes with where each came from, the design it is tied to, its origin (client or card) and its revision. An unknown planId is refused.
- `slicerx_design_rename_part` 1.0.0 renames an existing part of a design, as a step named "Rename <old> to <new>": it replays, saves and undoes like any step, so a part from an opened file or a piece a cut or split made ("Hull top top") can take a plain name. `part` is from 0 and `name` is taken plain (no space at either end, each run of spaces one space). An empty name is refused with "name: give the part a name", one longer than 120 characters with "name: keep the name to 120 characters (this one has N)", another part's name, in any case, with "name: part N is already called X; give this one a name no other part has", and the part's own name with "name: part N is already called X".
- `slicerx_app_part_rename` 1.0.0 renames one part of an object open in the app, as Rename in the object list (double-click or F2 on the part, or Rename in its menu), with the same name rules and refusals: one toolbar Undo, and with a CAD history it is the same step. The part's filament, print settings and paint stay with it. Returns the parts read back, with `changed` false when the part already had that name.
- `slicerx_design_part_filament` 1.0.0 sets which filament an existing part of a design prints with, as a step named "Filament N for <part>": it replays, saves and undoes like any step. `part` is from 0 and `slot` from 1 to 32; another slot is refused with "slot: give a filament slot from 1 to 32", and a part the design does not have with the number of parts it has. The answer is the design, with the part's new slot in `parts`, and the summary "Set <part> to filament N: step N". In the app the step clears a filament picked on the plate for that part, so the step's slot shows, and undoing the step puts the pick back.
- `slicerx_geom_rail` 1.0.0 adds a rail (a railing or fence) to a design in one call: a round post at every corner of a line (each turn sharper than 30 degrees, and an open line's ends), the rest spread evenly between corners no more than `spacing_mm` apart, and a flat top bar on them. The line is `path` (points on a plane, `closed` for a rail all round) or `face` (all round the outer edge of a flat face, `inset_mm` in from it). Posts and bar default to twice the nozzle, with the bar 0.2 mm wider than the posts so it caps them; thinner posts or bars, a bar not wider than the posts, or a rail no taller than its bar are refused with the size that works. The posts sink 0.05 mm into what they stand on. The rail is a new part of its own (`name`, `slot`) or, with `join`, part of the part it stands on. It adds two steps, "Rail posts, N" and "Rail bar", that `slicerx_design_undo` takes off together; a bar that does not go through takes the posts back with it. The reply's `rail` gives the part, the number of posts and how many stand on corners, the largest spacing between posts, the length and the sizes used.
- `slicerx_design_look` 1.1.0 (from 1.0.0), in the mimir tab, takes `compare`: the share of the whole-model view that changed since the last look at the design, drawn twice in one frame and measured, as `visiblePct` against `floorPct` (2) with `belowFloor`, and the picture as it is now.
- `slicerx_design_edit_step` 1.4.0 (from 1.3.0) takes `size` and `move`. `size` sets what a step made to a length along one axis, from its low end: an extrude's height or outline, a revolve along or across its axis, a loft along its run or across its sections. `move` shifts what the step made by [x, y, z] mm. Copies a pattern made of the step follow either one, a pattern of a pattern included, and paint laid on the part after the step follows what it painted. A step number sent as text ("2") is that step unless a step has that id or name.
- `slicerx_paint_preview` 1.3.0 (from 1.2.0) takes `whole`. In the mimir tab, a color asked for on a whole part already painted in height bands asks which band, the rest of the part, or all of it, as a one-tap question, and `whole` true skips it. A client with no way to ask is refused with the bands listed in words.
- `slicerx_design_edit_step` 1.5.0 (from 1.4.0) takes `section` for a loft step: reshape a lofted body (a car body, a fuselage, a bottle, a vase) by its stations. Change one station's width, height or place along the run (`station` with `width`, `height`, `scale`, `at`, `out`), add a station blended from the two around it (`add`) or take one out (`remove`), raise or drop the top or bottom line toward the ends (`line`), lean an end out (`end` with `lean`, `leanBy` or `overhang`), give the body more or less curve (`curve`) or a flatter bottom (`flatten`). Stations are named front, back, middle, widest, shoulder or all, and nose, tip, tail, rear, neck and base work too. Extrudes joined on the body's top and parts standing on it are moved up or down onto the new top at the steps that made them, and their paint goes with them. The answer lists what moved, and says when a part now hangs past an edge, sits into the body or stands clear of it, and when the body now needs supports. The edit is kept either way.
- `slicerx_design_steps` 1.3.0 (from 1.2.0) lists a loft step's stations front to back (number, place, width, height) and its run axis. When the design has a loft, the answer carries `shapeWords`: phrases people use for a body's shape ("fuller nose", "more overhang", "rounder shoulder") and the section edit for each.
- Sketch loops take an ellipse: `{"type": "ellipse", "center": [u, v], "widthMm": w, "heightMm": h, "angleDeg": a}`, its full axis lengths with the width axis turned `angleDeg` (default 0) counterclockwise from `u`. `slicerx_geom_sketch_check` 1.2.0 (from 1.1.0), `slicerx_geom_sketch_edit` 1.2.0 (from 1.1.0), `slicerx_geom_extrude` 1.10.0 (from 1.9.0, as `shape` `{"type": "sketch", "loops": [...]}`), `slicerx_geom_revolve` 1.9.0 (from 1.8.0), `slicerx_geom_loft` 1.7.0 (from 1.6.0), `slicerx_geom_sweep` 1.10.0 (from 1.9.0, as a profile) and `slicerx_design_sketch` 1.4.0 (from 1.3.0) all accept it. A design keeps it as given and replays it as a true curve. `slicerx_geom_sketch_check` reads it back like any loop: its `areaMm2` and `lengthMm` (perimeter) per loop. A missing or zero size is refused with the field named. `slicerx_geom_sketch_edit` mirrors and patterns an ellipse whole, its `angleDeg` turned with the copy; intersect, trim and extend refuse a sketch with one.
- `slicerx_geom_extrude` 1.10.0 takes the shape `{"type": "ellipse", "widthMm": w, "heightMm": h}`, centered on `placement.center` with its width along `u` before `placement.rotationDeg` turns it. It takes every extrude option, a taper included.
- `slicerx_geom_array` 1.5.0 takes `steps` with a design: the steps named (each by its index from 1, its id, or a plan feature's name for all its steps) are repeated on their own part in a row, a grid or around a circle, as one `pattern` step, instead of copying the whole part. The steps themselves are the first copy. The step's value is the count, so `slicerx_design_edit_step` changes it later. A step that makes a new body, works by side or height, or steps on different parts are refused with the reason, as are `merge` false, `rotateCopies` false and more than 100 copies. A copy that misses the part refuses the call with `Copy N:` and the reason, and the design is unchanged.
- `slicerx_geom_mirror` 1.3.0 takes `steps` the same way: those steps are mirrored through the plane onto their part, as one `pattern` step, and `copy` false is refused with them.
- `machine:prusa-core-one-indx`, the Prusa Core One with the INDX nozzle changer, written from Prusa's and Bondtech's public INDX pages until Prusa publishes its own profile: eight tools with 0.4 mm nozzles, a 248 x 205 x 270 mm volume, a 12 s tool change and extruder offsets of zero, with the plain Core One's motion limits and retraction. The printer profile search finds it, and `slicerx_slice_file` and `slicerx_estimate_file` slice a plate on it with one tool per filament: each color change is a `T` command and an `M109` wait for the new tool's temperature, never an `M600` manual swap, and the estimate counts 12 s per change. No tool's input or answer shape changes, so no tool version moves.
- `slicerx_geom_hole_edit` 1.0.0 changes a round hole already in a model or a design: a new diameter, a blind hole's depth, and a counterbore or a countersink at its entry. It takes the input that version 1.3.0 of `slicerx_geom_hole` took and gives the same answer, except that a pick that finds no hole now says a new hole is `slicerx_geom_hole`'s, and the `operationResult` of an edit by a topology reference names `geom.hole_edit`. With the app link on it runs in the open app as the `geom.hole_edit` method, edit class.
- `slicerx_geom_interference` 1.0.0 checks two parts of a design against each other. It takes `design`, `a` and `b` (part indexes, not the same one) and returns `interferes`, `overlap_mm3` (the volume the two share), `clearance_mm` (the smallest distance between their surfaces, 0 when they touch or interfere) and, when they do not interfere, `from` and `to` (the closest pair of points, one on each part, mm). A shared volume under 0.001 mm3 counts as touching. A gap beside a round face reads up to about 0.02 mm under the modeled one, since round faces are drawn as flat facets. A part that is not closed is refused with what is open. Read only. App link method `geom.interference` (read class).
- `slicerx_geom_fit_check` 1.0.0 measures the gaps between a design's bodies for print-in-place parts (hinges, pins, chains), with the engine's fit check. It takes `design`, optionally `part` (part indexes, each once, default all), `material` (a filament type such as PLA, PETG or TPU) and `layer_height_mm` (0.04 to 0.8 mm), and returns `limits` (`side_mm` and `vertical_mm`, the smallest gaps moving pieces keep per side for the nozzle, layer height and material, by the same fit rule as `fit_check`, with `material`, `nozzle_mm` and `layer_height_mm`), `pieces` (bodies that touch count as one), `bodies` (`body`, `part`, `name`), `gaps` and `warnings`. Two bodies can have a side gap and a gap above or below, each held to its own limit, so a tight gap over a pin is not hidden by a wider one beside it. A side gap within 0.02 mm under its limit counts as clearing it: a round pin and bore drawn as facets measure a little closer than they are modeled. So does a gap above or below between round faces, such as over a pin lying flat in its bore, even when the bore is drawn with few sides and the level strip along its top is over 1 mm wide. A level strip is the top or bottom of a round face only when the facets past it on both sides keep turning, a second one leaning further within 2 mm, so a pin or bore drawn in as few as 8 sides counts as round, while a strip with a chamfer on both sides, such as a drafted rib or a lead-in, is a flat face. A gap above or below under a flat face, over a level one (a rail or rib narrower than 1 mm counts), is held to its limit itself, since the fit rule rounds it down to whole layers. A strip under 1 mm wide on the face above is no flat face, even beside a wider face across a thin groove. Whether a face is flat does not depend on how the part is turned. Each gap has `bodies`, `names`, `kind` (`fused` for a touch or overlap, `horizontal` or `vertical` under the limit, `apart` for the closest gap of two pieces within 2 mm), `direction` (`xy` or `z`), `gap_mm`, `limit_mm` (the limit for its direction), `verdict` (`fuses`, `binds` or `slides`), `where` for a body resting on another ("on the top face of Tray"), and `from` and `to`. Each warning says what to change, such as "Lid and Tray touch on the top face of Tray, 0.0 mm; moving pieces need 0.4 mm or more". Read only. Its description says that `slicerx_design_check` of a plan with `printInPlace` waits for it. App link method `geom.fit_check` (read class).

### Changed

- `slicerx_geom_shell` 1.5.0 (from 1.4.0) shells a part of a design with exact solids on the exact kernel, where the engine has it: the wall is the thickness asked at every point, on a curved body too, and the part stays an exact solid. The faces the shell makes read as made by its step. A shell the kernel cannot close still runs on the mesh, and its step says so, as does any shell with a face open on a body with curved faces other than cylinders and cones, such as a lofted body open at its top. Before, every exact part was shelled as a mesh (voxels on a curved body) and was a mesh from that step on. The description says which bodies get exact walls.
- `slicerx_project_show` 1.4.0 lists `mixed_filaments` when the project has any, and each plate's slice carries the mixes its objects use, by part or by paint, as the `mixed_filaments` setting.
- `slicerx_project_add_filament` 1.3.0 refuses a filament number a mix holds.
- `slicerx_project_duplicate` 1.2.0 copies the filament an object was set to print with.
- `slicerx_project_object_nozzle` 1.1.0 counts an object on a mix as its two filaments.
- `slicerx_app_paint` 1.1.0 lists the project's mixes as `colorMixes`, and `slicerx_app_paint_preview` 1.4.0 takes a mix's number as a color state.
- `slicerx_design_plan` 1.25.0 (from 1.24.0): its `colors` field says a band that stands for a real feature (a stripe, a trim line, the line where one paint meets another) sits at that feature's real height scaled to the model, looked up first when it isn't known, and never as a thin token band in its place.
- `slicerx_paint_preview` 1.7.0 (from 1.6.1): its `target` says the same of a height band that stands for a real feature.
- `slicerx_app_paint_preview` 1.3.0 (from 1.2.0): its `target` says the same, as `slicerx_paint_preview` does.
- `slicerx_design_plan` 1.24.0 (from 1.23.0): its display build rule asks for a curved body's top line to follow the real body from end to end, as its bottom does, with the top height at both ends and in the middle given as the sizes topStart, topMiddle and topEnd, and the sections built to them. Before, a plan could give a curved body one height, and its loft kept a flat top along most of its length while only the bottom changed, which the check passed.
- `slicerx_geom_extrude` 1.15.0 (from 1.14.0) says where a join or cut that misses the part sits on a design with exact parts. Before, the step was refused with "The shape no longer reaches the part". Now the refusal gives the shape's box and the part's box in the design's own coordinates, as the design summary's bounds read: "The cut's shape sits at x 4 to 6, y 150 to 152, z -1 to 1, 140.0 mm from the part (x 0 to 10, y 0 to 10, z 0).", "..., under the part (...)" for a shape wholly below the part, or "..., 0.0 mm short of the part." when the two boxes meet. On a `frame` given without `u` it goes on with which way the frame's axes ran: "This frame's sketch u runs along -X and v along +Z; give frame.u to choose them." An axis that runs along none of X, Y and Z is named by its direction, such as `[-0.71, 0.71, 0]`. A design on the mesh still says how far short of the part the shape stops, as before.

- Slices report which filament each object's features printed with. `slicerx_slice` gives `featureFilaments` per plate and `slicerx_slice_file` gives `feature_filaments`: for each object, the filament slots its walls, infill, surfaces, support and other features printed with, after the project's and the object's own settings (sx engine). `slicerx-mcp call` reads it from a shell.
- `slicerx_design_sketch` takes `constraints` on its loops, dimensions among them: points by loop, place and segment (the sketch origin is loop -1), lines and circles by loop and segment. The app solves the sketch first, as its dimension tool does, so the loops move to meet them, and a dimension that would over-define the sketch is kept driven. The answer gives the degrees of freedom left and which dimensions are driven; constraints that conflict are refused and nothing is added.
- `slicerx_geom_sketch_edit` edits sketch loops as the sketch tools do: `intersect` lists where segments cross, `trim` takes a piece back to the nearest crossings and joins what is left to open chains its ends meet, `extend` runs an open end on to the next curve, `mirror` adds copies across a line and `pattern` adds copies along a step or round a center. It answers with the loops and, for each, the loop it came from.
- `slicerx_geom_sketch_solve` solves a constrained 2D sketch: points, lines, circles and arcs held by coincident, horizontal, vertical, parallel, perpendicular, tangent, equal, symmetric, distance, angle and the other sketch constraints. It answers with the solved points and radii, the degrees of freedom left, which points can still move, and the constraints that conflict or repeat.
- `slicerx_mimir_quota` reads today's free mimir use on this computer from the app over the app link: what the install and the network have used of their daily caps, in US dollars, and whether mimir cloud is open. `slicerx-mcp call slicerx_mimir_quota` reads it from a shell.
- `slicerx_printer_status` and `slicerx_printer_list` show what a printer reports wrong now (an HMS code or a print error) through sx-link: the first problem in the summary, a `problem` row with its code in the display, and `issues` in the output. Problems left over from an earlier job are left out.
- Project filaments: `slicerx_project_add_filament` adds a filament after the last one and `slicerx_project_set_filament` changes one's material type or color, as a person does on the app's filament rail. Both answer with the project, and `slicerx_project_show` lists its `filaments`. Once a client sets one, the project's slices carry `filament_type` and `filament_colour` for every filament.
- `slicerx-mcp call <tool> [<json>] ...` runs tools from a shell, in order in one process, and prints one JSON line per call; it stops with exit code 1 at the first failure.
- `slicerx_project_add_model` takes `bodies` for an STL that holds several separate bodies: `auto` (the default, one object as before), `parts` (one object with a part per body) or `objects` (an object per body). The answer lists each new object with its part count (`objects`, `object_count`, `part_count`), and `slicerx_project_show` gives every object's `parts`.

- `slicerx_printer_history` reads a printer's past jobs through sx-link, newest first: file name, result, start and end time, duration and filament used, as the printer keeps them. Read only; `limit` takes 1 to 200 (default 20). On simulated printers it says the history comes through sx-link.

- Placement and per-object control of the open project: `slicerx_project_move`, `slicerx_project_rotate`, `slicerx_project_scale`, `slicerx_project_plate_add`, `slicerx_project_plate_remove`, `slicerx_project_move_to_plate`, `slicerx_project_remove_object`, `slicerx_project_object_settings`, `slicerx_project_add_volume` (negative, support blocker, support enforcer or settings modifier, as a box, cylinder or STL), `slicerx_project_remove_volume` and `slicerx_project_height_ranges`. Each answers with the project after the change; `slicerx_project_show` now lists each object's rotation, scale, place, settings, volumes and model bounds, where every copy sits on its plate, and the height ranges. The sx engine slices with all of them.
- `slicerx_open_in_app` opens a model or project in the SlicerX desktop app on this computer, as the browser's Open in SlicerX button does.
- The checks and project operations the SlicerX app runs from its panels are tools now, so a client can do what a person does there: `slicerx_gcode_inspect`, `slicerx_risk_report`, `slicerx_energy_estimate`, `slicerx_fit_check`, `slicerx_mesh_analyze`, `slicerx_printer_config_check`, `slicerx_spool_fit`, `slicerx_overnight_readiness`, `slicerx_spool_inventory`, `slicerx_printer_match`, `slicerx_fleet_overview` and `slicerx_ams_mapping` read; `slicerx_supports`, `slicerx_orientation_search`, `slicerx_mesh_repair`, `slicerx_hollow`, `slicerx_emboss`, `slicerx_scale_with_tolerance`, `slicerx_threads_and_fits`, `slicerx_smart_layer`, `slicerx_multicolor_assign` and `slicerx_region_modifiers` change the open project (class slice). The checks read the plates `slicerx_slice` sliced; the mesh tools need `sx-geom`. None of them starts a print.

- Slice and estimate results carry `notices`, warnings with a code. `no_printer` says the slice named no printer, so the engine used its generic machine ("No printer chosen, so this slice used a generic 200 mm machine; times and limits are not for your printer."). The same line is in `warnings`.

- Set up local AI: `slicerx_local_ai_check` (hardware, one recommended model with its reason, size and license, running Ollama or LM Studio), `slicerx_local_ai_status` (installed local models and the one set up last) and `slicerx_local_ai_setup` (an Ollama download with progress notifications, then a tool call and speed check). Setup always asks the user first. Requests go only to 127.0.0.1. An edition config turns them off (`features.localAi`) or limits the models (`ai.allowedLocalModels`).
- A tool that must ask for a particular call (`mustAsk`) now asks over MCP even when its class is set to Allow, as it does in the app.

### Changed

- `slicerx_design_open` reads 3MF files with the engine's 3MF reader (sx-geom `format.read3mf`), the one the app uses, instead of a reader of its own. Parts keep their shared vertices, and a file in centimeters, meters or inches now places its build items in millimeters too. `readDesigns` takes the engine as its first argument and returns a promise.
- The `project_gcode_review` message is one short line and what to do ("edited.3mf's printer G-code doesn't match the Bambu Lab A1 profile's. ..."). The per-script counts and flagged lines stay in `details`, which adds `summary` (that first line) and `printer` (the printer it was compared with), so a client can show one line and keep the rest behind a toggle.
- A project with its own printer G-code and no printer to compare it with (no `machine:` profile, and a printer SlicerX has no profile for) is refused with the new code `printer_needed` instead of a diff against the generic machine's G-code, where nearly every line read as added. `project_gcode: "profile"` slices it as before.
- A project object whose mesh a tool replaced (cut parts, a repaired or hollowed mesh) keeps its geometry and rotation, so it slices; before, the plate came out without it.
- Project meshes are handed to the mesh tools with shared corners, so a closed STL measures watertight instead of showing every edge open.
- License: the server code is Apache-2.0. The bundle inlines the AGPL-3.0-or-later stock profiles, so the package is `Apache-2.0 AND AGPL-3.0-or-later`, with the AGPL text in `dist/LICENSE-AGPL-profiles.txt`.
- The published `./agents` entry is built (`dist/agents.js`), and both entries ship self-contained type declarations.
- Starting, resuming or sending G-code to a printer can no longer be approved through MCP. Those requests return `needs_person` and wait for a person in SlicerX or on the phone; `slicerx_approve` can only decline them. Pause and cancel still follow the policy. `slicerx_approve` ignores `bed_clear`.
- HTTP mode requires a bearer token on every request, loopback included. A new token is written to a 0600 file at every launch, or set with `SLICERX_MCP_TOKEN`. `--token` is refused, since command lines are visible to other users.
- In link mode a person-only request is registered with its work (`approvals.register {request, work}`), and the hub's `approval.done` report shows in `slicerx_pending_approvals`.
- HTTP mode uses MCP sessions, each with its own pending approvals, so a session can list and approve only its own requests.

### Fixed

- Windows command-line and MCP clients can read the remembered hub credential saved by the desktop app. App grants still control reads and edits; test editions use their own credential name.

- A call the server refuses before the tool runs (a tool it doesn't have, or arguments the tool's input schema rejects) reads plainly, over stdio and HTTP: "Tool slicerx_x not found" instead of "MCP error -32602: Tool slicerx_x not found". It is still a tool result with `isError`, and requests the protocol refuses are still JSON-RPC errors with their codes.
- `slicerx-mcp --printers link` as a stdio server now exits when the client closes stdin, after answering the requests it had already read. Before, its sx-link connection kept it running until something stopped it. With no printers or `--printers demo` it already exited.
- `slicerx-mcp --printers link call ...` now exits once its calls are done: 0 when every call worked, 1 when one failed. Before, it printed the results and then kept running with its sx-link connection open until something stopped it.
- `slicerx_region_modifiers` with `apply` now changes the open project: it adds a ring round each named hole and each named box, cylinder or sphere region as modifier volumes, and puts the feature-only settings on the object, as the app does. Before, over MCP it only returned the plan with "This project cannot hold volumes or object settings here, so nothing was applied." `slicerx_project_show` lists what it placed.
- `slicerx_printer_queue` sends a Bambu Lab printer the plate as a `.gcode.3mf` with a slot map, as the app's Print sheet does. Before, the plate went as plain G-code with no `ams_mapping`, and a P1S with an AMS stalled at the start. Without `slotMap`, each filament is fed from the loaded slot of its material and closest color, read from a fresh status. The call is refused before any card when a filament has no loaded slot of its material (`no_matching_slot`), when the printer has not reported its slots yet (`slots_not_reported`), or when two filaments would share a slot (`duplicate_slot`). The `needs_person` answer carries the `file` and `slot_map` handed to the hub, and a started print's result carries `slotMap` and `slotMapSource`.
- A slice or estimate that names no process now takes its supports from the printer, as a fresh plate in the app does: the maker's own Standard preset for the printer and nozzle where SlicerX ships one (off on Bambu Lab printers, with the preset's support style), and SlicerX's Standard tier's tree supports for no printer or a printer with no maker preset. Before, `process:slicerx-default` turned tree supports on for every printer. That profile no longer sets any support keys; a process profile, an Easy goal, a process preset file or an `enable_support` override still turns supports on.
- A Bambu Studio 2.8 project's `reduce_infill_retraction_mode` now reaches the engine as `reduce_infill_retraction`. It was dropped as a key only Bambu Studio has, so every travel inside the infill retracted. "Enabled" skips those retractions, "Disabled" keeps them, and "Auto" skips them when every filament's `filament_metal_stickiness` is low or untested (PLA) and keeps them otherwise (PETG). A file with only the old `reduce_infill_retraction` key reads as before. The app opens projects the same way.
- A travel through a printer's exclusion area or the A1's nozzle wrap check corner, or within the nozzle's width of one, is now refused with `collision` like a print path there, as a `keep_out` item in `details.collisions`. The server reads sx's sentence for it ("A print path or travel enters ...") as well as the older one.
- A Bambu Studio or OrcaSlicer project sliced with `project_settings` no longer fails on its stock start G-code (the Bambu Lab A1's `M211`, `M500` and `M18`). The project's printer G-code is compared with the printer's stock text, the template SlicerX ships or any version Bambu Studio or OrcaSlicer shipped for the model, and stock text is used as it is; the result's `project_gcode` says which. Other G-code stops the call with the new `project_gcode_review` code, with the diff against the printer profile's G-code and each flagged line in `structuredContent.error.details`. The new `project_gcode: "profile"` argument slices with the printer profile's G-code instead. No tool call can choose the project's own G-code.
- `slicerx_slice_file` and `slicerx_estimate_file` slice a 3MF plate as its objects, so `print_sequence` "by object" prints each one before the next, with the nozzle lifted over the finished ones. Before, the plate became one mesh and printed layer by layer. A file is placed on the printer's own bed (`printable_area`), `project_settings` takes a plate's own print sequence, and `slicerx_inspect_project` lists it per plate. A by-object plate whose objects sit too close for the toolhead, or with an object the gantry or lid would hit, fails with the new `sequence_clearance` code.
- A user's preset that inherits from a maker profile SlicerX ships (`profile_files`, or a slot in `filaments`) no longer makes the whole slice use the strict G-code checks. G-code it inherits is the shipped text; only G-code the file sets itself is checked strictly. Before, a user's PLA preset on a Bambu Lab A1 stopped the slice with `preflight_blocked`.
- `slicerx_slice_file` and `slicerx_estimate_file` no longer refuse the start G-code of shipped printer profiles (the Bambu Lab A1's, for one). Like the app, they mark the custom G-code as trusted only when no G-code setting came from a profile SlicerX does not ship, a project file, a preset file or an override; anything else still gets the strict checks. Projects sliced through mimir (`slicerx_slice`) follow the same rule.
