### 1. A model that cannot fit sliced for minutes before it was refused (night finding 14)

- Area: engine, app.
- Severity: medium. The app looks hung.
- Evidence: a 20 mm cube scaled 100 times (2 m, like a file in the wrong units) sliced in full and only then met the toolpath check; the test took 54 s in a debug build.
- Commit: fd7a042 `fix(core): refuse a model that cannot fit before slicing it`. Once the meshes are read, a model wider or deeper than the bed, taller than `printable_height`, or wholly off the bed is refused at once, with the same blocked error and words as the later check, naming the model, plus a hint when a tenth, 1/25.4 or a thousandth of its size would fit. The test now finishes in 0.03 s. A model partly over an edge is still left to the toolpath check.
- Orca: the canvas tracks instances fully or partly outside and warns (GLCanvas3D.cpp `ModelInstancePVS_Fully_Outside`, `EWarning::ObjectOutside`); a unit prompt only for models too small, at import (Plater.cpp). / Bambu: the same, from the same code. / Prusa: the CLI drops objects outside the print volume ("All objects are outside of the print volume", ProcessActions.cpp); unit prompt only for models too small (FileLoadingLogic.cpp, by volume). / ours: refused before slicing with a unit hint for oversize models, and units detected automatically at import, with undo (import-auto.ts), because a hint at the likely cause beats a model marked red, and the engine is also used without the app.

### 2. The auto filament map ignored the skirt (night finding 2, H2D and H2C)

- Area: engine, multi-material.
- Severity: medium. The plate was refused instead of printed.
- Evidence: a one-filament part at x 40 with a 15 mm brim and three skirt loops went to the right extruder (x 25 to 350), and its skirt at x 23.4 stopped the slice. With two filaments, the layer's first filament printed the skirt even when its extruder could not reach all of it (filament 1 left at x 60, filament 2 right at x 310: the skirt ran past x 325).
- Commits: e7c219b `fix(core): let the auto filament map count a one-filament plate's skirt` (the filament's box grows by the brim, skirt distance and loops, so the left extruder is chosen) and 90267de `fix(core): give the skirt to a filament whose extruder reaches it` (the first filament of the layer whose extruder reaches the whole skirt prints it). The brim part was fixed in the night (cut to the reach).
- Orca: the map checks only the objects' walls and fills (PrintObject.cpp `detect_extruder_geometric_unprintables`), the brim is cut to the reach (Brim.cpp `outer_inner_brim_area`), the skirt is not handled, and the skirt prints with the first extruder. / Bambu: the same code; its H2D profiles print no skirt. / Prusa: not handled (no per-extruder reach). / ours: the skirt counts in the map and goes to a filament that reaches it, because otherwise a correct plate is refused for a line that only primes the nozzle.
- Still open: supports printed past a part's footprint can leave the chosen extruder's reach. They are rare (normal supports stay within the part's footprint plus `support_expansion`) and the reach check blocks the file. Proposed: grow the support filament's box by its support extent in the map, the same way. Small in size (a few lines in nozzles.rs).

### 5. Grid infill broke into more strokes than Orca's (night finding 19)

- Area: engine (sparse infill joining).
- Severity: low to medium. The same lines and material, but more travels and retractions (print time, stringing).
- Evidence: path_parity `sparse-grid` (the block, 15 percent grid): 369 strokes against Orca's 92; on layer 10 Orca prints one stroke of 45 segments and SlicerX four. A trace of the joining on that layer showed the cause: every line end has a short arc (2.518 mm) to the line that crosses it near the wall and a longer arc (4.593 mm) to the next pair, and following the short arcs through the lines closes loops. Shortest first takes every short arc but the one that would close each loop, and the ends that leaves have their longer arcs already used, so each loop stays a stroke. Which arc of a loop is left out is decided by the order among the tied short arcs, and that order came from rounding noise in the lengths (2.5176 against 2.5176 plus a few nanometers). Replaying the same joining over 2000 random orders of the ties gave 1 to 6 strokes, most often 4 or 5, and one stroke only 4 times: Orca's one stroke on this layer is its integer lengths falling well, not a rule.
- Commit: 292f646 `fix(core): take infill arcs of one length in order along the boundary`. Arcs within 5 microns count as one length and are taken in order along their ring, which leaves one loop-closing arc per loop at a place where the longer arcs can still join the loops. Test: `patterns::tests::a_grid_in_a_square_joins_into_one_stroke` (five squares and layers; before: 3, 3, 1, 1 and 2 strokes, now 1 each).
- Parity after (sparse and anchor cases, strokes SlicerX/Orca, filament within 1 percent of Orca in every case and closer in most): grid 92/92 with 4140/4140 moves (was 369/92); crosshatch 167/387 (440), lateral honeycomb 124/325 (412), lateral lattice 194/379 (423), 3D honeycomb 257/347 (412), TPMS-D 529/866 (739), quarter cubic 179/192 (277); line, triangles, rectilinear and the zigzags unchanged. path_parity's stroke check is now one-sided (fewer strokes than Orca pass), with a guard so fewer strokes cannot hide bad joins: sparse infill moves that follow a wall (within 1 mm, within 20 degrees of parallel) are measured as connectors, the longest may not exceed Orca's by more than 5 percent plus 0.5 mm, and the total by more than 5 percent plus one connector of Orca's mean length per stroke saved (each saved stroke adds a join, so a plain 5 percent on the total would fail every improvement: crosshatch has 592 mm more connectors for 220 fewer strokes, 2.7 mm a join, under Orca's own longest). Commit: 7a6a422 `test(bench): let path parity pass fewer strokes and check the joins`. Old against new build over all 105 cases: grid, crosshatch and octagram spiral go from FAIL to ok; nothing new fails.
- Found by the guard, failing before and after this change (written up for later): the gear anchor cases; honeycomb's and 3D honeycomb's longest connectors (8.29 and 6.71 mm against 5.21 and 4.48); zigzag on the gear and the base gear case (connector totals 23 and 5 percent over); lightning and TPMS-FK (geometry differs already).
- Still open: the gear anchor cases (315 strokes against 195) are unchanged; their arcs do not tie, so the cause is elsewhere in the joining.
- Orca: shortest first with `std::sort` by length alone (FillBase.cpp `connect_infill`), so ties fall as the sort leaves them. / Bambu: the same code. / Prusa: the same code (its origin). / ours: ties taken in order along the boundary, because the number of strokes should not depend on rounding, and the order along the ring leaves the loops joinable.

### 6. Printer scan and the Windows Firewall (night finding 32)

- Area: Windows, printer connect.
- Where it stands: the in-app hint shipped in the night (a scan that finds nothing on Windows names a closed firewall prompt and points to entering the IP address). Discovery listens for Bambu Lab SSDP and mDNS announcements on inbound UDP, which Windows Firewall gates per program; adding a printer by IP connects outward and needs nothing.
- Orca: not handled (no rule; a generic "check your network and firewall" on cloud errors). / Bambu: not handled (the same message on cloud and plug-in download failures, GUI_App.cpp and DownloadProgressDialog.cpp). / Prusa: not handled. / ours: the hint only.
- Options:
  - The NSIS installer installs per user (Tauri's default `currentUser`), without admin rights, so it cannot add a firewall rule. Adding one would mean elevating the installer (a UAC prompt for every install and update) or a per-machine mode: a real change to install and update behavior, for one feature.
  - The MSI installs per machine and could add a program exception for private networks with the WiX Firewall extension. That takes a custom WiX fragment and the extension in the bundle step: an installer and signing-pipeline change, and only MSI users would get it. A rule an installer adds also outlives a user's own choice to block.
  - In the app, with no installer change: when a scan finds nothing on Windows, read the firewall's rules for the app's own program (`netsh advfirewall firewall show rule name=all dir=in verbose`, readable without admin rights, started with no console window) and, when a Block rule or no Allow rule exists for it, say exactly that: "Windows Firewall blocks SlicerX from hearing printers. Allow it under Windows Security, Firewall, Allow an app, or enter the printer's IP address." It would be a few dozen lines of Rust in the desktop crate, a few KB of binary and nothing in the web bundle, with no new dependency.
- Chosen: the in-app check, read-only, with no installer change.
- Commit: 9c27dde `feat(desktop): say when Windows Firewall keeps printers out of a scan`. When a scan finds nothing on Windows, the desktop app reads the enabled inbound rules for its own program through PowerShell's firewall cmdlets (their enum names are not translated, unlike `netsh` output; no admin rights; no console window; the program path is sent encoded so no character in it can change the command). A Block rule on private networks, or no Allow rule, turns the hint into a plain statement ("Windows Firewall blocks SlicerX from hearing printers on this network") with an "Allow in Windows Firewall" button that opens Windows' own Allowed apps page. When the rules allow the app, the firewall is not mentioned. Nothing is ever added silently. The printer bridge runs inside the desktop app, so its program is the one checked; a standalone sx-link is not. Size: about 150 lines of Rust and 40 of TypeScript; nothing in the WASM.
- Orca: not handled. / Bambu: not handled. / Prusa: not handled. / ours: the app reads its own rules and names the cause, with Windows' page one click away, because an empty scan with no reason is the first thing a new Windows user meets and a silent rule change would override their own choice.

### 7. Anchor limits under 0.05 mm still joined lines (from the joins guard)

- Area: engine (sparse infill joining), test harness.
- Severity: low. Hooks a few hundredths of a millimeter long on every line end when the limit is set that small.
- Evidence: the new joins guard failed `anchor-zero-gear` (`infill_anchor_max` 0) with 164 mm of connectors against Orca's none. Every sparse stroke in that case is one straight line, so nothing was joined: the guard had counted short whole-line stubs in the gear's teeth that run beside the tooth flanks (there are more of them than in Orca's because the lines fall at a different phase on the gear). Checking Orca's handling of a zero limit turned up the real difference: Orca reads any `infill_anchor_max` under 0.05 mm as "do not connect" (FillBase.hpp `FillParams::dont_connect`, applied in Fill.cpp after `anchor_length = min(anchor, anchor_max)`), while SlicerX ran the joining for any limit over 0, so 0.04 mm gave 0.04 mm hooks on every line.
- Commits: 6b23f3a `fix(core): join no infill lines when the anchor limit is under 0.05 mm` (in `anchor::connect`, so lightning and adaptive cubic, which call it directly, follow too; test `patterns::tests::an_anchor_limit_under_a_twentieth_of_a_mm_joins_nothing`) and 67fec7f `test(bench): count no connector in a stroke of one move` (a single-move stroke is a lone line). `anchor-zero-gear` now passes with 0.0/0.0 mm of connectors; all 105 cases: 80 ok, 25 FAIL, no other case changed.
- Orca: under 0.05 mm, no joins and no hooks (`dont_connect`), lines only chained. / Bambu: the same code. / Prusa: the same (`dont_connect`). / ours: the same threshold, because a limit that small cannot make a useful join and the setting reads as off.
