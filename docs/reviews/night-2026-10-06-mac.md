## Findings

### 1. Absolute extrusion over-extrudes after an A1 timelapse block (severe, fixed in f830369)

- Area: G-code safety, custom G-code.
- Evidence: fuzz case `bambu-a1__cube20__absE__3` (stock A1 profile and maker G-code, `use_relative_e_distances` false). From layer 3 the timelapse block's indented `M83` (inside `M622 J1`) put the printer in relative mode, and the rest of each layer kept writing absolute positions (`G1 X118.996 Y132.726 E60.07591`), so one move fed up to 65 mm. New test `absolute_extrusion_survives_custom_gcode_that_switches_to_relative` (tests/firmware.rs) fed 41,660 mm against 847 mm relative before the fix; equal after.
- Fix: `absolute_e` in src/gcode.rs reads indented lines, turns `M83` into `M82`, tracks `G92 E`, and leaves `G91` moves relative.
- Orca read: GCodeWriter.cpp (`preamble`, `reset_e`), GCode.cpp custom G-code insertion. Orca passes the block through unchanged, so it has the same fault.
- Who is hit: anyone who turns relative E off on an A1 or A1 mini (the Bambu Lab default is relative, so not the default path).

### 2. Abrasive filament on a soft nozzle never warned (medium, fixed in 453b139)

- Area: safety warnings, parity.
- Evidence: every stock profile has `nozzle_hrc` 0, and packages/app/src/adapters/profile.ts deleted `required_nozzle_HRC` in that case, so Bambu PET-CF on a P1S (stainless, HRC 20 against 40) was silent. Orca warns. The engine also warned "nozzle not known" on every CLI slice of Bambu PLA (HRC 3) when `nozzle_hrc` was missing, though `nozzle_type` said stainless.
- Fix: `preflight::nozzle_hardness` runs after slicing, counts only filaments with extrusion, falls back to `nozzle_type`. Tests: preflight unit test, tests/safety.rs `carbon_fiber_on_the_stock_stainless_nozzle_is_reported`, app profile-layer test.
- Orca read: GCodeProcessor.cpp (`NOZZLE_HRC_CHECKER` block), Print.cpp `get_hrc_by_nozzle_type`, PrintConfig.cpp `nozzle_hrc`.
- Left: with several nozzles the softest counts; Orca checks each filament's own extruder.

### 3. Unretract at the park spot after a timelapse that ends a layer (low, written up)

- Area: retraction and travel.
- Evidence: A1 raft or support-only layers (no outer wall), and every empty layer: the timelapse block sits at the end of the layer, then the writer unretracts (`G1 E0.8`) at the park position (`X0 Y128` on the bed edge, or `X261 Y250` over the layer 3 clog check) and the next layer retracts again at once. A small blob at the park spot. Cause: src/gcode.rs, the `Spot::End` loop re-primes because every chunk must end unretracted so shards concatenate.
- Why not tonight: the "chunks end unretracted" rule is what makes shard joins byte-identical. Changing it touches the shard contract. Suggested fix: let a chunk end retracted when its last block is a park, and record that in the layer marker so the next chunk skips its first retract.

### 4. A 753 byte 3MF hangs the loader (medium, fixed in f62c1cf)

- Area: crash paths, file import.
- Evidence: a 3MF whose objects each hold the level below four times, 16 levels deep (4^16 cube copies). `sx slice bomb.3mf` ran 40 s and reached 1.7 GB before it was killed. `Mesh::load` serves the CLI, the desktop slicing command, the WASM build and the cloud upload scan. The app's own project import (packages/app/src/export/import3mf.ts) follows one level of components only and is not affected.
- Fix: a work budget per file in src/threemf.rs (`MAX_WORK`, 40 million: one per object visited plus vertices and triangles per copy). The same file is refused in about a second. Test `components_that_repeat_a_mesh_without_end_are_refused`.
- Also run: 1,500 mutated copies of the two benchmark 3MF projects (numbers swapped for -1, NaN, 2^32, huge; cut, duplicated and spliced XML) through `sx metadata`, `sx slice --plate 1` and `sx slice`. No panic, signal or timeout.

### 5. Multi-color prints refused on the Qidi X-Plus 4 (high for that printer, fixed in 4e3b3ac)

- Area: multi-material, profiles.
- Evidence: the two-color X mark on every printer through the request path (`trustedGcode`): all 20 X-Plus 4 runs failed with "the prime tower (35 x 16 mm) does not fit on the bed clear of the objects". The profile's `bed_exclude_area` is 13 points that trace two corner strips joined along the bed's edges; src/tower.rs `no_go` took one bounding box round them, the whole 305 mm bed. packages/app/src/plate/arrange.ts `excludeBoxes` did the same for arrange.
- Fix: a flat list longer than four points is one zone per four points (engine, viewport, arrange), zero-area groups dropped. After: 172 of 172 two-color runs on all printers slice with stock and tree supports.
- Orca read: slic3r/GUI/PartPlate.cpp `calc_bounding_boxes` (a box per four points, a short tail dropped).

### Fuzz runs with no finding

- 1,334 single-color runs (30 edge-case meshes x 46 variants, P1S): no panic, no G-code fault. Refusals were all expected (empty mesh, oversize, too tall, inside the P1S cutter corner).
- 7,224 runs on all 43 shipped printers (6 meshes x 19 variants x 1 and 3 copies) through the request path: no panic. The only flagged G-code was finding 1 (absolute E) and flush or park moves outside the bed that the makers' own G-code makes on purpose (A1 X261 Y250 clog check, X267 flush; P1P X20 Y-3 chute).
- 860 two-color runs: the X-Plus 4 refusal above; by-object refusals where the two touching parts are too close, as expected.

### 6. Spiral vase with several objects printed only one (high, fixed in 5247327)

- Area: engine correctness, silent wrong output.
- Evidence: three 20 mm cubes on a P1S in vase mode, layer by layer: the G-code labels object 3 on all 100 layers and objects 1 and 2 on three layers each, no warning. src/session.rs keeps only the largest outline of a vase layer (`spiral::largest_contour`), so the other objects keep their solid base and lose every wall.
- Fix: the plate is refused with `spiral_mode` unless it prints by object. Test `spiral_vase_with_two_objects_needs_print_by_object` (tests/firmware.rs).
- Orca read: Print.cpp `Print::validate` (the "By object" refusal for spiral vase, issue 4043).

### 8. Absolute extrusion pulled back 23 mm during every Bambu flush (high, fixed in b23c40e)

- Area: G-code safety, multi-material.
- Evidence: after fix 1, a two-color P1S plate still fed 14,812 mm in absolute mode against 13,995 mm relative, with single pushes of 39.8 mm. The filament change G-code renders `G1 E0.357503 F50` (six decimals from `{(flush_length_1 - 23.7) * 0.02}`); `parse_e` in src/gcode.rs refused more than five decimals, so the line stayed as written and the printer read it as a position: back from 23.7 to 0.36, then 23 mm extra on the next line. Firmware-simulated comparison of the same plate in both modes, per line, found the first divergence there.
- Fix: values round to five decimals. After: 13,995.8 against 13,995.3 mm on P1S, X1C, A1, A1 mini, P2S, H2S. Test `absolute_distances_take_extrusions_with_more_than_five_decimals`.

### 9. Spiral lift left the bed near its edge (high for Klipper printers, fixed in 785aba8)

- Area: retraction and travel, G-code safety.
- Evidence: every engine-written move (custom G-code excluded) checked against the bed on all printers: a plate of small parts with Spiral Lift had 855 moves on a Prusa MK4 and 452 on a Voron Switchwire up to 6 mm past the front edge (`G1 X83.162 Y-4.961 Z1.057`). Klipper stops a print on a move outside its position limits (whether the Switchwire's `position_min` is below 0 was not checked). No other engine move left the bed on any printer.
- Fix: `spiral_fits` checks the turn's circle against the printable area polygon; it lifts straight up when the circle does not fit. Test `a_spiral_lift_that_would_leave_the_bed_lifts_straight_up`.
- Orca read: GCodeWriter.cpp `lazy_lift`, `travel_to_xyz` (the "check the arc move all in bed area" to-do), `_spiral_travel_to_z`.

### 10. Spiral vase on a painted object printed a wall through the vase (medium, fixed in 6e99e13)

- Area: engine correctness, multi-material.
- Evidence: a cylinder with half its side painted filament 2, vase mode, P1S: 99 tool changes, both filaments used, each color's region printed as its own outline including the dividing line, no warning.
- Fix: refused with `spiral_mode` when the object uses more than one filament. Test `spiral_vase_refuses_a_painted_object` (tests/requests.rs). Checked after the fix: the same plate is refused, the non-vase plate still slices.
- Orca read: Print.cpp `Print::validate` (the "more than one materials" refusal).

### 11. Organic supports crashed with a branch angle of 0 (medium, fixed in 2021a47)

- Area: crash paths.
- Evidence: a sweep of tree support settings to their edges on the table, mushroom and sphere: `tree_support_branch_angle_organic` 0 (inside the accepted 0 to 85) exits 101 with "min > max, or either was NaN. min = 0.0, max = -0.0001" from `organic::settings::Settings::new`: the slow angle was clamped to `0..angle - eps`. The desktop app slices in `spawn_blocking` (apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/slicing.rs), so the slice fails with a panic error there; in the browser a panic traps the WASM worker.
- Fix: the upper bound stops at 0. Test `organic_trees_take_a_branch_angle_of_zero`.
- Orca read: Support/TreeSupportCommon.hpp (`support_tree_angle_slow`, the same `std::clamp`, which is undefined behavior there).
- Also checked: every other `clamp` with computed bounds in sx-core and sx-geom by reading; the rest are guarded (bounds ordered by construction or by an earlier check).

### 7. Two-color plates on single-nozzle printers without a changer (open question, written up)

- Area: multi-material.
- Evidence: the two-color X mark on a Prusa MK4, a Creality K1 and a Voron 2.4 (no MMU, one `nozzle_diameter`) slices with `T0` and `T1` tool changes and two filament totals. Orca clamps an object's filament to the extruders the printer has (Print.cpp, the commented-out "assigned an extruder that the printer does not have" check, and `Printer::apply` clamping), so the same plate prints in one color with no `T1`. Marlin answers `T1` with "Invalid extruder"; Klipper reports an unknown command.
- Why not tonight: whether a single-nozzle printer should get a manual color change (`M600`), one color, or a refusal is a product call, and the app may already map filaments to the printer's slots before the engine sees them. Worth a decision.
