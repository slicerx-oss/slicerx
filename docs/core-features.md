# Core engine features

What the SlicerX engine must do to replace Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer and PrusaSlicer for everyday printing. All of it is our own implementation, with no code from Orca. Setting keys keep the Orca-compatible names so imported profiles work. Each item lands with tests, G-code validity gates, native and WASM parity, and a comparison against an OrcaSlicer 2.4.2 reference build where one applies.

P0 is what every user touches in a normal week. P1 is what power users expect. P2 rounds it out.

## Priorities

- Print sequence: by layer (default) and by object, with gantry and extruder clearance checks for by-object, per plate.
- Most requested by OrcaSlicer and Bambu Studio users: vertical brick and interlocking layers, per-feature filament and full filament overrides, Z anti-aliasing contouring, tall print speed throttling, binary G-code.
- Color painting: slice per-triangle painted regions (the 3MF paint data Bambu Studio and OrcaSlicer write) into per-extruder regions, including painted seams and supports; the viewport gets brush, fill, smart fill and height-range tools.
- Default sparse infill pattern is gyroid (settings defaults, Easy mode and our process presets).
- Multi-color and AMS: filament slots per plate, mapping model colors and 3MF painted regions to AMS or MMU slots, swapping a color or slot without re-painting, tool change G-code per firmware (Bambu AMS, Prusa MMU3, Klipper and toolchangers), purge volume matrix with auto flush from colors, prime tower, flush into infill, support or object, filament change at a layer (M600) for single-extruder color swaps, and filament totals per slot. The app side (slot mapping, color swap, painting UI) follows once the engine supports it.

## P0: everyday printing

Walls and shells
- Wall count, wall order (inner then outer, outer then inner, inner outer inner), wall direction.
- Variable-width walls (Arachne style) and classic walls; thin wall detection; minimum feature and wall widths.
- Top and bottom shell layers and minimum shell thickness; ensure vertical shell thickness.
- Only one wall on top surfaces and on the first layer.
- Wall and infill overlap.

Infill
- Sparse patterns: rectilinear, line, grid, triangles, stars, cubic, adaptive cubic, gyroid, honeycomb, 3D honeycomb, concentric, cross hatch, lightning.
- Top and bottom patterns: monotonic, monotonic line, rectilinear, concentric, Hilbert curve, Archimedean chords, octagram spiral.
- Density, angle, per-layer rotation, anchor length, infill every N layers, solid infill threshold area, internal solid infill pattern.

Supports and adhesion
- Normal supports (grid and snug) and tree supports (organic, slim, strong, hybrid).
- Threshold angle, top and bottom Z distance, XY distance to object, interface layers and pattern, interface spacing, on build plate only, remove small overhangs, bridge without support.
- Support enforcers and blockers (painted and from modifier volumes).
- Skirt (loops, distance, height, draft shield), brim (outer, inner, both, auto, mouse ears), raft layers.

Bridges and overhangs
- Bridge detection with bridge flow, density, speed and fan; thick bridges; internal bridges over sparse infill.
- Overhang detection by degree with speed tiers; slow down for overhangs; reverse on odd layers for overhang walls; extra perimeters on overhangs.

Seams
- Aligned, aligned back, nearest, rear, random, back; painted seams; seam gap; wipe on loops; scarf seams.

Travel and retraction
- Retraction length and speed, deretraction speed and extra length, retract on layer change, retract only when crossing walls.
- Z-hop: normal, slope, spiral; lift above and below limits; when to hop.
- Wipe while retracting; avoid crossing walls (combing inside the part).

Speed, acceleration, cooling
- Per-feature speeds (outer wall, inner wall, small perimeters, sparse and solid infill, top surface, gap fill, support, support interface, bridge, travel, first layer, first layer infill).
- Per-feature accelerations and jerk or junction deviation; max volumetric speed cap per filament.
- Minimum layer time slowdown, fan by layer time, first layers fan off, overhang and bridge fan overrides, auxiliary and chamber fans.
- Temperatures: first layer and other layers, bed per plate type, chamber.

Gap fill and small areas
- Gap fill with minimum length; small area infill flow compensation.

G-code output
- Firmware flavors: Marlin 2, Klipper, RepRapFirmware, Bambu (.gcode.3mf packaging), Prusa (binary .bgcode and plain), Smoothie.
- Start, end, layer change, before and after layer change, tool change, filament change, pause and template G-code with a placeholder language (variables, conditionals, math) compatible with the common placeholder syntax.
- Object labels for cancel-object: EXCLUDE_OBJECT (Klipper), M486 (Marlin and Prusa), Bambu labels.
- Thumbnails embedded per printer (PNG, JPG, QOI; several sizes).
- Accurate time estimate using the firmware's motion limits; per-feature time; filament length, grams and cost.
- Arc fitting (G2 and G3), relative or absolute E, G-code substitutions, post-processing script hook.

Plates and objects
- Multiple objects and plates, instances, per-object and per-part settings, modifier volumes, negative volumes, height range modifiers.
- Auto arrange on true outlines and auto orient (sx-geom has both; see True shape nesting).
- Sequential printing (by object) with gantry collision checks.

Multi-material and color
- Tool changes for AMS and MMU style units, purge volume matrix, prime tower (wipe tower), flush into infill, object or support.
- Per-object and per-part filament; painted color regions from 3MF; color change at a layer height (M600 or firmware equivalent) and pause at height.

Quality details
- Elephant foot compensation, XY hole and contour compensation, slice closing radius, resolution, precise Z height.
- Pressure advance per filament and adaptive pressure advance per feature; flow ratio per feature.
- Ironing: top surfaces, topmost only, all solid; pattern, speed, flow, spacing, angle.

Preview data
- Per-move data for every color scheme: feature type, speed, flow, width, height, layer time, fan, temperature, tool; travels, retractions, wipes and seams as markers.

## P1: power users

- Spiral vase mode with smooth transitions (done); fuzzy skin (outside only, all walls); make overhangs printable (conical).
- Painted fuzzy skin (Orca 2.4.2: `fuzzy_skin_segmentation_by_painting` in MultiMaterialSegmentation.cpp, `apply_fuzzy_skin_segmentation` in PrintObjectSlice.cpp, the painted regions of PrintApply.cpp, `apply_fuzzy_skin` and `group_region_by_fuzzify` in Feature/FuzzySkin/FuzzySkin.cpp; src/fuzzy.rs `painted`, `ring_in`, `wide_in`, session.rs `fuzzy_mask`). The 3MF triangle attribute `paint_fuzzy_skin` (state 1) is read into `MeshPart::fuzzy_paint`. On each layer the painted part's outline is split by that paint the way color paint splits it (`paint::LayerPaint::split`), and the painted pieces are the layer's fuzzy area. A wall the print's own fuzzy skin already roughens keeps that; otherwise the outer walls of outlines and holes (Orca sets a painted region to `all`) get fuzzy skin only on the stretches inside the area: the ring is cut where it crosses the area's outline, the inside edges get the fuzzed points and the crossing points stay where they are, as Orca's `split_line` keeps the ends of each clipped stretch. `fuzzy_skin` = `disabled_fuzzy` (Orca's default, "Disabled") keeps paint off; `none` ("Painted only") roughens painted areas and nothing else. Orca warns in the paint tool that painting does nothing while fuzzy skin is disabled and offers a link to turn it on; SlicerX turns the object to Painted only on the first fuzzy stroke and says so. Tests: `fuzzy::tests::a_painted_area_roughens_only_the_stretch_inside_it`, `painted_fuzzy_skin_roughens_only_the_painted_side` (classic and variable-width walls), app paint.test.ts.
- IDEX copy and mirror modes; toolchangers with per-tool offsets; ooze prevention and standby temperatures.
- Soluble support interface materials; support first layer expansion.
- Polyhole conversion; precise outer wall; alternate extra wall.
- Infill combination across layers; sparse infill with its own filament; top surface density.
- Input shaping aware acceleration limits; firmware retraction (G10 and G11).
- Pause, color change and custom G-code inserted from the layer slider.
- Built-in calibration prints wired to results: temperature tower, flow rate, pressure advance (line, pattern, tower), retraction tower, max volumetric speed, VFA, input shaper test (sx-geom has the models).
- Incremental re-slicing with a stage cache so a setting change re-runs only the affected stages.
- Large meshes (millions of triangles) fully parallel.

## P2: completeness

- Non-planar top surfaces (experimental).
- Brim painting, per-layer settings from the slider, plate-level G-code overrides.

Walls that still land more than two line widths outside the outline's bounds are refused and the island gets fixed-width walls (`strays`). The x-mark and two-color x-mark benchmarks give the same G-code as before.

Support that must reach the bed stays tight.
- Lightning infill (src/lightning.rs, our own implementation of Cura's lightning method as Orca follows it: overhangs per layer, distance-field points, tree growth, pruning, straightening, reconnection; planned once per session over the sparse areas of all layers, `lightning_*_angle` keys) and adaptive and support cubic (src/adaptive.rs: an octree of corner-standing cubes split around the model's triangles, walls cut per layer). Parity (`pattern-*` in parity.py): lightning sparse infill 295 against 328 mm (total -1.7 percent, before 1878 mm as plain lines); adaptive cubic 1247 against 1334 mm (-6.5 percent, before +27); support cubic 1187 against 1315 mm (-9.7 percent). The lines are printed separately (Orca joins lines with hooks, which only changes travel). Adaptive and support cubic use the octree of all objects of the plate together. Not done: `fill_multiline` for these patterns.
- Print by object (`print_sequence` = by object): each object prints completely before the next, in plate order, each with its own first layer, brim and supports. The first layer of every later object lifts above everything printed, travels, then comes down, and the custom G-code templates see `print_sequence` and a `max_layer_z` that stays at the tallest finished object (Orca's `m_max_layer_z`), so the A1 family's timelapse and head wrap blocks leave out their trip to the bed edge as they do in Orca. Before, the templates saw "by layer" and the later object's own height, and the A1 timelapse ran the nozzle at layer height through the finished object. The plan is refused (`Error::Clearance`, objects named) following Bambu Studio's `sequential_print_clearance_valid` (Print.cpp), the printer maker's own rule, over Orca 2.4.2's: two convex hulls closer than `extruder_clearance_radius` (each hull grows by half of it less 0.1 mm), or, when every object is under `nozzle_height`, closer than the outer nozzle diameter of 4 mm (Orca's `is_all_objects_are_short`); a skirt around each object (`skirt_type` perobject) that reaches past half the radius widens the gap by its `object_skirt_offset`. An object that prints before another must stay under `extruder_clearance_height_to_rod` when the gantry passes over it while a later object prints (their y spans, each grown by half of `extruder_clearance_dist_to_rod`, overlap), and under `extruder_clearance_height_to_lid` otherwise. Orca 2.4.2 grows only by half the radius here; Bambu's rod distance is the measured one. A 3MF plate sent to `sx slice --request` without a transform is split into its build items (`api::load_3mf_plate_objects`), placed as the file places them with the plate centered on the bed, so by object and its checks see the objects. Parity run `parity_sequence.py` (tall and short box): 130 layers, same layer heights in order and same object order as Orca, filament -2.2 percent, walls equal, brim -4 percent. Tests: `tests/requests.rs` `print_by_object_*`, `tests/firmware.rs` `by_object_templates_*`, `cli/tests/request.rs` `a_3mf_plate_slices_as_its_objects`, `packages/mcp/test/print-sequence.test.ts`. Temperatures, fans, retraction and z-hop come from the plate's settings. Prime tower (`enable_prime_tower`, `prime_tower_width`, `wipe_tower_x`, `wipe_tower_y`, `prime_volume`, `flush_volumes_matrix`, `flush_multiplier`): a wall loop on the first layer and a purge zigzag after every tool change on every layer up to the last change, feature Prime tower; filament on the tower within 3 percent of Orca's prime tower (type 1) at the default prime volume. The matrix is used when the profile has one and is not checked against Orca, whose command line ignored it here. Not done: painted 3MF regions, flush into infill, objects and supports, per-slot tool change temperatures at the tower.
- Painted colors (3MF `paint_color` on triangles, Bambu and Orca; `slic3rpe:mmu_segmentation` is read the same way), src/paint.rs. The split-triangle layout was fitted to Orca with hand-written codes and random nested ones (`fit_paint.py`: zero mismatches on every parent and child layout and on 7 of 8 random trees). On a layer, painted walls color the region nearest them (a wedge behind a painted face), reaching back only `0.8 mm x (j - 1)` when the layer is the `j`th from a top or bottom surface within the shell, so the paint never shows through; painted top and bottom faces color their outline on the shell layers, drawn in 0.8 mm per layer. Parity on a 20 x 20 x 10 mm box (`probe_color.py --paint`, Orca on the P1S profile): painted right face, outer and inner wall on both tools within 4 percent and the wedge edge inside 0.1 mm; painted top face within 4 percent on walls, solid infill and top surface; painted bottom face within 5 percent. Distances are on a 0.1 mm grid (coarser above 4 million cells), so a color boundary inside a part is exact only to about 0.1 mm. Not done: Orca's choice of tool order per layer (sx still alternates by layer parity, so a layer that adds a color after a one-color layer costs one extra change: 51 against 49 on the painted-face box), interlocking, `mmu_segmented_region_max_width`, internal bridges over a color change (sx bridges, Orca does not), painted slanted faces (only walls and exactly flat faces are checked against Orca).
- Preview extras (src/extras.rs, SXPV flag bit 1, layout in docs/architecture.md): per segment the fan speed, nozzle temperature, G-code line in the file written, and flags for a retraction, a lift and a seam (the start of a closed wall loop), plus retract and lift flags per travel. They are read back from the G-code text and matched to the toolpaths by position, so they describe the file exactly (`preview_extras_point_at_the_gcode_line_of_every_segment` checks that the line of each segment ends where the segment ends, on the reference plate in 3 shards). Absent for binary G-code and when no G-code is written. Object index per segment (SXPV flag bit 2, after the extras): the index in the request's objects, from the object outline that holds the path's first point (the smallest when outlines overlap, else the nearest); 0xFFFF for the skirt, the prime tower, custom G-code and a brim around several objects; written for plates of two or more objects, so single object goldens are unchanged. Not done: extras for arcs (a fitted arc gives all its segments the line of the arc), bed temperature and acceleration.
- Tool order per layer (src/session.rs `plan_tools`): each layer starts on the tool the layer below ended on and ends on one the layer above uses, from the height each part and each painted surface covers. Tool changes now equal Orca's on the painted right, top and bottom face boxes and the two-box case (49, 6, 4 and 51 commands). Prime tower placement: the tower stays where `wipe_tower_x` and `wipe_tower_y` put it when that is on the bed and clear of the objects, else it moves to the nearest clear spot on a 5 mm grid, and the plan is refused when it fits nowhere. A profile's `change_filament_gcode` (the Bambu M620 sequence was rendered in full) sees `old_filament_temp`, `new_filament_temp`, both feed rates, `flush_length_1` (all of the purge as one length) and `max_layer_z`. `flush_into_infill` lets the new filament's sparse infill take up the purge (it prints first, the tower purges the rest); not checked against Orca, whose command line ignored the key here. Not done: `flush_into_objects` and `flush_into_support`, one skirt shared by objects that have their own settings, the bridge over a color change (sx prints 10 mm of T0 internal bridge under a painted top face where Orca prints none).

- The travel into a layer (`GCode::travel_to`, `needs_retraction`): sx wrote every layer without knowing where the layer below ended, so the first travel always retracted and lifted. - Variable-width wall order (`WallToolPaths::getRegionOrder`, `PerimeterGenerator::process_arachne`): sx printed every wall of one inset across the island before the next inset, and every middle bead last. Seam placement as Orca's SeamPlacer (Orca: GCode/SeamPlacer.cpp `SeamPlacer::init`, `process_perimeter_polygon`, `calculate_polygon_angles_at_vertices`, `compute_global_occlusion`, `raycast_visibility`, `calculate_point_visibility`, `calculate_overhangs_and_layer_embedding`, `SeamComparator`, `pick_seam_point`, `pick_random_seam_point`, `align_seam_points`, `find_seam_string`, `find_next_seam_in_layer`, `place_seam`; ShortEdgeCollapse.cpp `its_short_edge_collpase`; TriangleSetSampling.cpp; Geometry/Curves.hpp `fit_cubic_bspline`; src/seamplan.rs and src/raycast.rs). The whole object is planned once per session before the parallel stages (like supports), so shards read the same seams. Every layer's outer wall is a ring of candidates (the slice pulled in half the outer wall width, contours and holes); a candidate has the signed turn of the wall there read between points a nozzle diameter apart, its visibility, how far it hangs past the layer below (`distance + 0.65 w - tan 45 degrees * h`, and `unsupported_dist`), and for layers with several regions its depth inside the union (more than 0.5 mm inside is hidden). Visibility: the parts are merged and decimated to 16000 triangles by collapsing short edges (random order, edge allowance growing from 0.2 mm), 30000 points are sampled by area with `std::mt19937_64` seed 27644437 (the generator is tested against the standard's 10000th value), 5 by 5 rays leave each sample over its hemisphere and each ray whose nearest hit is a front face takes a 25th off, and a candidate reads the weighted mean of the samples within the radius that holds four samples with 90 percent odds. The comparator is Orca's (overhang first, hidden points, rearmost for `back`, then overhang plus visibility plus 0.6 times the angle penalty, 1.0 times and a distance term for `nearest`); ties go to the first candidate, and the candidates of a ring start at its lowest, leftmost vertex going counterclockwise, which is where a Clipper ring starts and so where Orca's `pick_seam_point` starts (this took `back` on the mushroom from 0 to 100 percent: its stem is a rectangle and the two rear corners are equal). The random numbers are the library's: the decimation shuffle is libc++'s `std::shuffle` over `std::uniform_int_distribution` on `std::mt19937_64` (a draw masked to the bits of the range, rejected while it is not below it), which is what Orca's macOS build runs, so a mesh over 16000 triangles collapses the way Orca's does when its topology matches. `aligned`, `aligned_back` and `back` then link the picks of neighboring layers into strings (seams within four line widths of the last one that score within 0.3 of their ring's own pick, up and down, with the 20 alternative starts) and, for strings of at least six seams, fit a weighted cubic B-spline over height (4 mm per segment; `back` strings use 0.3 of the length) and move each seam to the fitted point, kept on its own corner by a weight that grows with the corner's sharpness. `random` is Orca's number from the first point's position, spread along the edges of the points as good as the best; `nearest` picks per ring with the nozzle position. A wall ring finds its candidates by the nearest point and starts at the seam, inserting a vertex when the seam is not on one; an inner ring takes the outer seam projected onto itself, into the corner when the outer seam sits in a concave one. Painted seams keep their own path (seam.rs). Skirts and brims keep the old rule. Outputs: transcendental functions are computed in double and rounded, so native and WASM builds make the same choices (`check-wasm` on a seam fixture). Parity (`bench/compare/seam_stats.py` on the kept G-code of the `seam-` cases: share of loops whose seam lies within one line width and within 1 mm of Orca's, drift = distance between the seams of consecutive layers): `aligned_back` x-reference 62 and 91 percent, gear 50 and 97, mushroom 78 and 78; `aligned` mushroom 100 and 100, x-reference 17 and 19; `back` mushroom 100 and 100, gear 49 and 55; drift matches Orca on every model (aligned 0.13 against 0.13 mm on x-reference, 0.43 against 0.38 on the knot; random 13.6 against 13.6 and 5.5 against 5.5), which says the strings are as straight. What does not match is a choice between equal corners: symmetric parts (gear teeth, the flare, bars with two equal ends) are decided by Orca's sampling noise and by where its polygon starts, and the knot has more than 16000 triangles, so its decimation (a `std::shuffle` we do not reproduce) differs. `nearest` reads the nozzle position within a layer only, since layers are planned independently (Orca continues from the layer below). Painted enforcers and blockers are candidate kinds in the planner (0.2 mm oversampling, central enforcer, enforcer weights in the string fit; seam.rs only serves skirts and brims) and negative volumes open the occlusion mesh (a hit counter over all hits). Not done: `staggered_inner_seams`.

Support Z gaps (src/config.rs). Floating regions (src/floating.rs, raised in `api::build_session` when support is off). Orca checks support necessity by running its tree overhang detection (`PrintObject::is_support_necessary`: sharp tails, cantilevers over 6 mm) and names the object only. SlicerX checks the mesh instead, which costs little: a lowest point or lowest flat face of a part above the first layer with no material of any part within 0.5 mm under it is a floating region; a patch of downward faces flatter than `support_threshold_angle` whose farthest point lies more than 6 mm from the material of the layer under it is a long overhang. Long bridges (src/floating.rs `classify`, raised with the floating regions). Orca leaves a bridge without support up to `max_bridge_length` (default 10 mm) and supports a longer one (`PrintObject::remove_bridges_from_contacts` in PrintObject.cpp: a straight unsupported stretch landed on the layer below at both ends, and `stBottomBridge` surfaces whose bounding box is under the limit, are taken out of the support contacts); it never warns about them. SlicerX tells the person before slicing for supports: a patch of flat downward faces, less what the layer under it holds (grown 0.2 mm, as Orca grows the lower layer by half a line), is crossed in the bridging direction (`bridging::direction`, Orca's `detect_bridging_direction`) by strands at most 0.5 mm apart. When every strand lands on the layer under it at both ends, the patch is a bridge, not an overhang: a bridge up to `max_bridge_length` is fine and a longer one raises `LongBridge` ("long_bridge") with its layer, place, span and the fix (supports, or slower bridges with more fan). A patch with any strand that hangs free at an end stays a long overhang as before. The plate risk check shows these with thin walls and floating islands, each with its fix (packages/pilot/skills/risk_report `engineRisks`, the app's risk-fixes.ts). Tests: `floating::tests` (an 8 mm bridge is fine, a 40 mm one is a bridge, one with a free end is an overhang), `a_bridge_past_the_max_bridge_length_is_named_when_support_is_off`.

Organic tree supports (src/organic/, Orca 2.4.2 `TreeSupport3D` in Support/TreeSupport3D.cpp, Support/TreeModelVolumes.cpp and Support/TreeSupportCommon.hpp, with `generate_interface_layers`, `generate_raft_base`, `generate_support_layers` and `generate_support_toolpaths` from Support/SupportCommon.cpp). ## True shape nesting (arrange and fill the bed)

Arrange and Fill bed nest the real outline of each object (sx-geom `packages/geom/src/nest`, app side `packages/app/src/plate/nest.ts`). An L tucks into another L's corner and a washer goes inside a ring; parts turn by any multiple of the turn step.

- Outline: `nest.footprint` projects every triangle of the object's meshes, after its transform, onto the bed and unions them. Holes and inner corners stay, overhangs count, and holes under 1 mm2 are filled. The outline is simplified by up to 0.1 mm in a way that only grows it, and the exact convex hull is kept for the fit against the bed edge, so a part that exactly fits the bed still fits.
- Room around each part: the margins arrange already used (`footprint.ts`). Brim, raft and size compensation keep parts apart (two brims plus 1 mm, or the gap when that is more); everything printed, skirt and support pad included, keeps clear of the bed edge, the excluded areas and the prime tower; with a skirt or draft shield round the whole print, the hull of the print stays off the excluded areas.
- Placement: for each part and turn, the free region is the rectangle its reference point may take on the bed minus the no fit polygons of everything already there (fixed objects, excluded areas, the prime tower, parts placed earlier). Every vertex of that region is a position where the part touches something, and a score picks one. A no fit polygon is the union of the pairwise sums of the two outlines' convex pieces (ear clipping, then Hertel-Mehlhorn merging), unioned in a tree; its holes are the places where one part fits inside the other's hole. They are cached by the turn between the two parts. Grown outlines are closed by the smallest half gap when that gives fewer pieces, which loses no position since no part fits a narrower slot.
- Turns: multiples of the turn step (10 degrees by default; 5, 15, 30, 45 and 90 in the arrange options). Turns that give the same outline (a ring, a square, a gear tooth pitch) are tried once. Twelve coarse turns come first, then the steps next to the best two.
- Search: passes over orders (largest first, longest first, the largest one or two last so more parts fit when not all do, shuffled), scores (rows from the front, smallest envelope, square growth, columns) and turn sets (all, quarter turns, half turns), the passes that win most often first. The best layout places the most parts, then the most area, then has the smallest envelope. The search stops on a budget of counted work, not time, so a plate gives the same layout every time on any machine, in the browser and natively (byte for byte on all 20 benchmark plates). Arrange options offer Quick, Normal and Thorough. The status line shows the layout being tried once a run passes 300 ms.
- Fill bed places as many copies as fit (up to 100 at a time) around everything on the plate. Arrange all centers the layout on the bed when nothing fixed is in the way.
- The bounding box packer (`arrange.ts`) stays for pasting, new copies and new primitives, and is the fallback when the geometry engine cannot run.
